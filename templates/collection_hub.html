Send the right reminder

Check the current balance before sending. Start with a friendly due-date message, then follow up on any balance that remains. Use the family's preferred language and leave disputed amounts for a private conversation.

Your end-of-cycle checklist

  • Every bill has an amount, service period, due date and payment method.
  • Every recorded payment has been matched to your records.
  • Each receipt identifies the amount received this time.
  • Unpaid balances, credits and disputed items have clear notes.
  • Saved documents are accessible only to people who need them.

Four collection mistakes this workflow prevents

  • Reminding before checking. A reminder sent on a stale balance — after a payment arrived but before it was entered — costs more trust than a late one. Check the record first, every time.
  • Issuing a receipt for a report. A PAID message starts a check; only a verified payment earns a receipt. Keep the two states separate in your records.
  • One vague total. A single unexplained total invites an argument. Tuition, an assessed fee, a credit and verified payments as separate lines settle most questions before they are asked.
  • A different process per person. When follow-up lives in one staff member's phone, it leaves when they do. One written workflow — bill, remind, verify, reconcile — survives sick days and staff changes.

Common questions

What five details does every tuition bill need?

The family or account label, the service period, the agreed amount, the due date, and how to pay. Keep the document number on your payment record so a receipt can always be matched back to the bill it belongs to.

What is the difference between a reported payment and a verified payment?

A reported payment is a family telling you they paid — a PAID text, a note at pickup. A verified payment is the amount you have matched in your own payment records. Receipts and balances change only on verified payments.

How do partial payments work in this workflow?

Record each partial payment separately, with its own date and reference. The receipt shows the amount received this time; the balance is the bill minus every verified payment, as the worked example on this page shows.

How often should I reconcile tuition records?

At the close of every billing cycle at minimum: compare bills and receipts against the payments that actually arrived, and give every unpaid balance, credit and disputed item a clear note and a next follow-up.
