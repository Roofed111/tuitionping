Daycare tuition collection guide

How to get daycare tuition paid on time

Make the amount, deadline, payment method, and follow-up predictable. This checklist gives you a process to adapt without changing how families pay.

Prepared by TuitionPing · Updated October 6, 2026

1. Put the tuition agreement in writing

Identify the billing period, tuition amount, due date, cutoff time, and accepted payment methods. Define grace days, any late-fee trigger, and how to request an arrangement. Use the editable late-fee policy template to start; adapt it to your agreement and applicable rules before relying on it.

Use an actual example alongside the wording. If tuition is due October 1 and five full calendar grace days follow, the first fee-bearing day in that example is October 7, not October 6.

2. Give families one clear way to find payment details

Every reminder should state your program name, the amount, the deadline, and how to pay. For a payment link, check that it opens on a phone. For cash or checks, state where and when you accept them. Do not ask families to find an old newsletter to work out the payment method.

3. Remind before tuition is overdue

A three-day heads-up and a due-day message are useful starting points. Customize the timing to your agreement and avoid sending a due reminder after a verified payment. Use the English and Spanish tuition reminder texts, with your program's details replacing every placeholder.

Example

For monthly tuition due October 1, a three-day heads-up is September 28. For a weekly bill due Monday, October 5, it is Friday, October 2. The free templates can be used manually; confirm your account's billing options before relying on an automated schedule.

4. Separate payment reports from verified receipt

When a parent says they paid, acknowledge the message and check your records. Confirm the amount, billing period and destination before issuing a receipt. Bank transfers may take time to appear; a message alone is not settlement confirmation.

In TuitionPing, a PAID reply is parent-reported. The provider verifies the payment. In a spreadsheet, record the parent's message separately from the verified-payment amount.

5. Reconcile balances before following up

Record the bill amount, approved credits, assessed fees, and verified payments. Compare your tracker with your bank or payment-system records. The free tuition payment tracker includes separate columns for those amounts and a review date for overdue status.

For example, $300 tuition plus a $10 assessed fee, minus a $20 credit and $100 verified payment, leaves $190. Remind the family about $190, not $310.

6. Use your actual policy when a balance is late

Check whether the grace period has ended and whether a fee is allowed by your agreement and applicable rules. The calculator can explain the arithmetic, but it is not your payment ledger or a determination that the fee is permitted.

Record any waiver or payment plan, including who approved it and the next due date. A consistent process can include documented exceptions; it does not require ignoring a genuine billing error or a family's circumstances.

7. Assign the next action

Give one staff member responsibility for checking payments and reviewing overdue accounts. Log the last contact and the next follow-up so two people do not send conflicting messages. For repeated lateness, use the private follow-up playbook.

A short review checklist

  • Did a payment arrive that has not been entered?
  • Is the amount and due date correct?
  • Is there a credit, waiver or arrangement?
  • Has a reminder already been sent?
  • Who owns the next action, and when?
