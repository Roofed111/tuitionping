What should a daycare late payment letter include?

Use the program and family name, service period, original due date, itemized balance, verified payments, payment instructions and a clear date to pay or contact you. Check for a payment in transit before sending the notice, and share it privately with the responsible adult.

A partial-payment example

An $800 tuition charge plus a $25 fee already assessed, less a $25 credit and $200 in verified payments, leaves $600 outstanding. The letter requests that remaining $600; it does not claim the full $800 is unpaid.

Can I print or save a PDF?

Yes. Create the letter, then choose Print / save as PDF. Your browser's print dialog offers the available printer and PDF options. Only the letter prints. Keep a copy with your records; closing or reloading the tab clears the form.

Does this send the letter or add late fees?

No. This is a manual writing tool. It does not email or text families, assess fees, update your account, or process payments. Review any fee against your existing written agreement and applicable requirements. The letter is not legal advice, a collection demand, or a suspension or termination notice.

When is a reminder enough?

A short text can be useful for an upcoming due date. A written letter is useful when an overdue balance needs an itemized explanation or a documented follow-up. See how to handle late-paying parents for a private, practical approach.
