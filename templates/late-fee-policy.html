The policy

Copy, customize, and include with enrollment:

Late payment policy — [Your Program Name]

Tuition is due on the [1st] of each month. A grace period of [5] days applies — payments received after the [6th] are considered late.

A late fee of $[25] will be added to any account with an unpaid balance after the grace period. The late fee applies automatically each month the balance remains unpaid.

Accounts more than [30] days past due may result in suspension of care until the balance is paid in full. Returned payments incur a $[35] fee.

Payment methods accepted: [online link / check / cash]. Questions about your balance? Just ask — we're happy to help.

Template only — not legal advice. Late-fee rules vary by state and contract; have your agreement reviewed by a local attorney before you enforce it.

How to pick your numbers

Grace period (3–5 days). Short enough to matter, long enough to feel fair. Weekends and bank holidays cause most "late" payments — a few days absorbs them.

Fee amount ($25–$50). High enough to change behavior, low enough that no one quits over it. Flat fees beat percentages for daycare — they're easy to explain and easy to automate.

Escalation point (30 days). State it now, in writing, so the conversation later is about the agreement. Most owners never need it — the written line does the work.

Making it stick

Collect it at enrollment. A policy signed on day one is ten times easier to enforce than one introduced mid-year.

Apply it every time. The first waiver teaches every parent that the fee is negotiable. Consistency is the whole policy.

Let the system be the bad guy. When the fee is automatic — added by software, stated in the agreement — it's not you vs. the parent. It's just how the program works. (TuitionPing adds your late fee automatically after your grace period and tells the parent in their next reminder.)
