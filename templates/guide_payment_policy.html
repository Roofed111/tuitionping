Daycare payment policy template

One written policy that answers every money question before it becomes a pickup-line negotiation: how much, when it's due, how to pay, what happens if it's late, and how to ask for an arrangement. Copy the template below, replace every bracket, and give it to families before enrollment.

Prepared by TuitionPing · Updated October 20, 2026

How to use this template

1. Replace every bracket with your program's real terms. Delete any section you don't offer — a shorter policy families actually read beats a long one nobody does.

2. Check it against your agreement. If your enrollment contract already promises a grace period, a notice window or a fee, the policy must match it word for word on the numbers.

3. Have families sign or acknowledge it at enrollment, and re-acknowledge when terms change. Keep the signed copy with the child's file. This template is a starting draft, not legal advice — adapt it to your licensing rules and state requirements.

The template

[Program name] — Tuition Payment Policy

1. Tuition and billing period. Tuition for [child's program/classroom] is $[amount] per [week/month], covering care from [start date]. Tuition is charged for the place reserved, not for days attended, unless a credit is approved in writing under section 5.

2. Due date. Tuition is due on [day/date, e.g. the 1st of each month] by [cutoff time]. If the due date falls on a day the program is closed, tuition is due on the [last open day before / next open day after] it.

3. How to pay. We accept [check payable to ___ / bank transfer / the payment link we send you]. Please write [child's name] on every payment so it is credited to the right family.

4. Grace period and late fee. Payments received within [5] full calendar days after the due date are on time. After the grace period, a late fee of $[amount] is added to that bill. [Our full late-fee wording is in the late-fee policy you signed.]

5. Absences, holidays and closures. Tuition is [not reduced / reduced as follows: ___] for absences, family vacations, program holidays and emergency closures of up to [number] days.

6. Payment arrangements. If paying on time becomes difficult, contact [name] before the due date. We may agree a written arrangement with specific amounts and dates. An arrangement replaces the usual due date only for the bills it names.

7. Returned payments. If a payment is returned unpaid, [a fee of $___ applies / we will ask you to pay by a different method for the next bill]. Contact us promptly so we can agree the next step.

8. Changes to this policy. We will give at least [30] days' written notice before changing tuition amounts, due dates or fees. Changes take effect on the date stated in the notice.

9. Ending care. If an unpaid balance remains after the steps above, we will follow the notice procedure in your enrollment agreement.

Acknowledged by: ______________________ (parent/guardian)   Date: __________

Worked example: the same policy, filled in

A home daycare charges $950/month, due on the 1st by 6 p.m., with five full grace days and a $25 late fee. In that policy, a payment arriving October 6 is still on time; the fee can first be added to a bill unpaid on October 7. The tuition collection checklist walks through the same day-counting, and the late-fee calculator checks the arithmetic for your numbers before you publish them.

Notice what the example does not do: it does not repeat the fee every day or every week, and it does not promise a credit for absences the policy doesn't offer. Whatever your template says is what the family will hold you to — write the policy you actually run.

Introducing the policy to families you already serve

Send it with a short cover note, not buried in a newsletter: what is changing (or that nothing is changing — you are putting existing practice in writing), the effective date, and where to ask questions. Give the full notice window your current agreement promises. When only the amount is changing, the tuition increase letter templates give you the wording for that specific note.

After families have the policy, your reminders can stay short. A text that says "tuition of $950 is due October 1" works because the grace period, the fee and the payment method are already in writing — see the reminder text templates for the message side of the same system.

Common questions

What should a daycare payment policy include?

The tuition amount and billing period, the due date and cutoff time, accepted payment methods, any grace period and late fee, how absences and holidays are treated, how to request a payment arrangement, and how much notice you will give before the policy changes.

When should families get the payment policy?

Before enrollment, and again whenever a term changes. A reminder text should point back to terms the family has already seen — the policy is the agreement, the reminder is only a nudge about it.

Can I change my payment policy for current families?

Generally you give the notice your current agreement promises — 30 days is a common example — and have families acknowledge the new terms in writing before the effective date. Check your agreement and the rules that apply to your program.

Should the late fee live in the payment policy or a separate document?

Either works if it is written, specific and given to families in advance. Many programs keep the full late-fee wording in the payment policy and use a shorter standalone late-fee policy as the handout families sign.

Is this template legal advice?

No. It is a starting draft for a childcare program's own terms. Adapt it to your agreement, licensing rules and state requirements, and have important changes reviewed before you rely on them.
