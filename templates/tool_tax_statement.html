Year-end tuition statements, explained plainly

What is a daycare year-end tax statement?

A short document from the childcare provider summarizing what a family paid for care during the calendar year, with the provider's name, address and tax ID number. Parents use it when they claim the child and dependent care credit or document dependent care FSA expenses. There is no official IRS form for the statement itself — providers issue their own, which is what this generator makes.

What should it include?

Your program's name and address, your EIN or other tax ID number, the parent's and child's names, the tax year, the total the family paid for care that year, and the date you issued it. A care period and your contact details help parents match the statement to their records.

A worked example

A family paying $950 a month for a full year of care paid $11,400. If they joined in March and paid for ten months, their statement total is $9,500 — the statement always reports money received in that calendar year, not the contracted rate. Why do parents need my EIN?

IRS Form 2441, used for the child and dependent care credit, asks for the care provider's name, address and taxpayer identification number. Parents generally cannot claim the credit without it. Share the finished statement privately with each family, the way you would any document containing a tax ID.

When should I send statements?

In January, after the year ends and before parents file. Build each statement from your payment records for the full calendar year — not from invoices alone — so the total matches money you actually received.
